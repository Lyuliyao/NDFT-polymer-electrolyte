**SI Appendix, Fig. S-W (Section S8; formerly main-text Fig. 5) | Learned kernels in real space, ε_r = 7.5.**
The cancellation in a is the generic behaviour of a direct correlation function inside a region the particles do not reach; it is a consistency check of the learned functional. Main-text Fig. 5 shows where the concentration dependence sits.
**a**, Effective pair interaction between ions at c = 0.04: the real-space kernel W_αβ(r) of the second functional derivative of F_ex^θ at the uniform state (the negative direct correlation function in units of k_BT) plus the bare Coulomb interaction l_B e_α e_β/r (dotted). Inside the solvation shell the learned part cancels the Coulomb part: the cation–anion curve is flat within 1 σ instead of −40 k_BT at contact, and like ions repel each other far less than 1/r. The shaded region r < 0.5 σ lies beyond the wavevectors carried by the training profiles (k ≲ 5 σ⁻¹) and is a basis extrapolation.
**b**, Anion–anion kernel W_−−(r) of the neural functional at c = 0.01, 0.04 and 0.08 (light to dark) and of the pair closure, which is concentration independent by construction (dashed). The contact well (−34 to −38 k_BT σ³) is shared; a positive shoulder at 1–2 σ grows with concentration. This channel carries most of the concentration dependence at ε_r = 7.5 (81% of the rise of Σ_αβ W_αβ(k₁), which sets Γ − 1); at ε_r = 2 both like-ion channels move (SI Appendix, S8).
**c**, Cation–cation kernel W_++(r), same conventions; it barely moves with concentration (contact −33 k_BT σ³), whereas the pair closure compensates for its missing density dependence with a deeper well (−70 k_BT σ³) and a shoulder.
