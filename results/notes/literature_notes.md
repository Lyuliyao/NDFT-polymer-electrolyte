## 2. Facts per paper

### Pesko2016 (Solid State Ionics 289, 118)
- System: six aliphatic polyesters with systematically placed ether, ester and carbonyl oxygens, plus PEO (5 kg/mol), all with LiTFSI, at nine salt concentrations up to 0.28 Li per monomer; conductivity by ac impedance 25-130 C, T_g by DSC.
- Method: experiment; VTF analysis to factor out segmental motion; a new concentration variable rho = Li per monomer (9 backbone atoms per monomer for every polymer).
- Claims: sigma/sigma_max and Delta T_g collapse onto master curves in rho; the maximum conductivity is at "rho = 0.22 +/- 0.02 for all polyesters and PEO" (p. 121); Delta T_g = 67.7 rho; VTF activation energies are similar (7.1-10.7 kJ/mol) so vertical offsets reflect the prefactor A.
- Key physics statement for us: the non-monotonic conductivity is attributed to charge-carrier increase at low salt and then "screening effects become important with increasing salt concentration, and this reduces the number of 'effective' charge carriers" (p. 120-121); above rho = 0.2 solvation sites saturate and the reduced conductivity plateaus.
- The PEO/LiTFSI conductivity maximum is quoted at r = [Li]/[EO] = 0.085 (p. 121, citing Lascaud1994).
- Relevance to our manuscript: experimental motivation. The salt concentration dependence of transport in PEO-type electrolytes is governed by ion-ion and ion-polymer correlations that the authors can only parametrise (A, E_a); no structural theory is offered. Our c = N_+/N_bead = 0.01-0.08 lies in the regime where these correlations turn conductivity over.

### Kuhlmann2026 (ACS Electrochem. 2, 1598)
- System: LiTFSI/PEO in a Li|electrolyte|Li symmetric cell at 90 C, polarised at 1.25 V/cm; operando WAXS at DELTA BL9 and a lab source; Hermans orientation function.
- Method: experiment testing the hypothesis that PEO chains align under polarisation (a candidate explanation for discrepancies between measured and modelled concentration and velocity profiles).
- Claim: null result; "no significant polymer chain alignment is induced in LiTFSI/PEO electrolytes during cell polarization" (Conclusion), so "transport parameters extracted under equilibrium conditions constitute a valid input for continuum models" (Conclusion).
- Framing facts we can reuse: polymer electrolytes "are not dilute solutions" (p. 1598); the continuum (Doyle-Fuller-Newman) description needs "the temperature- and concentration-dependent conductivity, diffusivity, and transference number, as well as the thermodynamic factor" (p. 1598); transference numbers from Balsara-Newman vary strongly with concentration and can be negative, eNMR gives about 0.2 (p. 1598-1599); the reference-frame issue (Shao et al., their ref. 15).
- Relevance to our manuscript: (a) an equilibrium free-energy functional is the right object because the electrolyte keeps its equilibrium structure under battery-relevant fields; (b) the thermodynamic factor is an input of the continuum models, and we obtain it (Gamma = 2n/S_NN(k_1)) from the functional without training on it.

### Aqvist1990 (J. Phys. Chem. 94, 8021)
- System: alkali (Li+, Na+, K+, Rb+, Cs+) and alkaline-earth cations in SPC water (also TIP3P for Na+), SCAAS spherical boundary, no cutoffs.
- Method: FEP/MD; Lennard-Jones ion parameters calibrated to absolute hydration free energies and first-peak ion-oxygen distances.
- Claims: parameters reproduce Delta G_hyd within about 1 kcal/mol (Li+ -122.2 vs -122.1 kcal/mol, Table I, p. 8022) and RDF peaks; gas-phase monohydrate energies come out reasonably; "the most reliable way to obtain parameters for use in simulations of solutions and biomolecules is to calibrate the calculated absolute hydration free energies against observed ones" (abstract, p. 8021).
- Relevance to our manuscript: it is the source of the Li+ Lennard-Jones parameters used in the OPLS-based LiTFSI/PEO force fields (Liang2024 ref. 31). Background for the all-atom lineage only; nothing on correlations or functionals.

### CanongiaLopes2004 (J. Phys. Chem. B 108, 16893)
- System: triflate and bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide (TFSI) anions, plus pyrrolidinium and alkylammonium cation charges.
- Method: OPLS-AA-compatible force field; ab initio geometries, torsions (the S-N-S-C profile of TFSI) and ESP charges; "validated against crystal structures and liquid-state densities" (abstract, p. 16893); densities overpredicted by about 3% (Conclusion, p. 16897-16898).
- Relevance to our manuscript: defines the TFSI model (the CL&P force field) used by Thum2021 and Liang2024, i.e. the atomistic anion our coarse-grained anion of diameter 1.6 sigma stands in for. Background only.

### Thum2021 (J. Phys. Chem. C 125, 25392) and its SI
- System: PEO (40 chains, 63 monomers) with 128 Li[NTf2], EO:Li = 20:1, between graphite-like electrodes with surface charge 0, +/-0.5 and +/-1 e/nm^2, 423 K, 1 us production; OPLS-AA plus CL&P with ionic charges scaled by 0.8 (p. 25393).
- Method: all-atom MD; density profiles, coordination numbers, free-energy profiles F(z) = -k_B T ln rho(z), spatially resolved displacement variances.
- Claims: strong component-specific layering; Li+ reaches the negative electrode only at -1 e/nm^2 (desolvation from PEO); layering creates free-energy barriers, 6.4 k_B T (second to first Li layer) and 8.4 k_B T (first to second) at -1 e/nm^2 versus about 2 k_B T for in-plane hops ("Free Energy Profiles" subsection); perpendicular Li+ mobility is reduced by a factor 5 (neutral, positive) to 20-25 (negative); a "true bulk regime is not present" even 3.5 nm from the surface ("Perpendicular to the Electrodes" subsection).
- The SI (skimmed) contains the density profiles rho(z)/rho_bulk (Fig. S1), coordination numbers, xy density maps and residence times; no structure factors or free-energy functional.
- Relevance to our manuscript: the atomistic picture of the ion density response to a planar external potential (charged wall), i.e. exactly the observable our functional predicts, but obtained by brute-force MD at one composition; the barrier language maps onto our delta F_ex / delta n. Also documents the 0.8 charge scaling that a learned functional would absorb into its kernels.

### Timachova2015 (Macromolecules 48, 7882)
- System: PEO/LiTFSI, M = 0.6-100 kg/mol, r = [Li]/[EO] = 0.01-0.08, 363 K.
- Method: PFG-NMR self-diffusion of 7Li and 19F; Nernst-Einstein conductivity compared with literature impedance data.
- Claims: NE conductivity from D_+ and D_- agrees with measured sigma at r = 0.02 and r = 0.08, "indicating that the salt is fully dissociated in these PEO/LiTFSI mixtures" (abstract, p. 7882); t_+ about 0.5 at low M, plateau above 10 kg/mol, insensitive to r; D_+ and D_- collapse on s = Li per chain with D - D_p proportional to 1/s; plateau ratio D_p,-/D_p,+ about 5 (p. 7886).
- Bjerrum-length statement: "lB = 7.5 nm in PEO, assuming a dielectric constant of 7.5" (p. 7886), used to argue that at low salt the anion motion is synchronised with the coordinated cation.
- Relevance to our manuscript: (a) motivates the dielectric constant epsilon_r = 7.5 and hence the large Bjerrum length of the coarse-grained model (our l_B = 7.96 sigma); (b) the claim of full dissociation with large l_B is exactly the situation where mean-field (Poisson-Boltzmann) electrostatics is wrong and polymer-mediated ion correlations matter; (c) their "not simple" relations between D_+, D_-, D_m and t_+ in concentrated solution point at the thermodynamic factor we compute.

### Tsamopoulos2023 (SI of the ACS Macro Letters paper; the parent paper itself was not in the set)
- System (SI Sec. S1, p. S-2 to S-3): 400 chains of 30 beads, LJ units, epsilon = k_B T at 400 K, sigma = 0.7 nm; bead diameter 1.0 sigma, cation 0.4 sigma, anion 1.6 sigma; WCA for ion-ion, ion-monomer and bonded pairs, LJ cut at 2.0 sigma for non-bonded monomers (needed for the glass transition); FENE bonds K = 30, R_0 = 1.5; cation-monomer solvation potential of Hall and co-workers, S_ij = 4.33, r_c = 5.0 sigma; Coulomb with epsilon_r = 7.5; PPPM; Nose-Hoover NPT at p = 0; LAMMPS.
- Concentration mapping: 2 c_s^LJ = c_s^real = [Li+]/[EO] (p. S-2); T = 1.0 maps to about 450 K after matching T_g of the neat melt (T_g = 0.426 reduced, 192.3 K) (p. S-5).
- Findings in the SI: T_g rises with salt; D_+, D_-, D_COM all fall with c_s (Fig. S2, p. S-6); the cation-polymer g(r) peak is much higher than the cation-anion peak, which "leads to seclusion of the cation from forming ion pairs and clusters with the bulky anion in our model system" (p. S-6/S-7); the monomeric friction grows exponentially to super-exponentially with c_s (Fig. S5); three VTF fitting schemes fit the conductivity equally well with wildly different parameters, "strong evidence that the VFT fitting for the conductivity in salt-doped polymers lacks solid physical basis" (p. S-11/S-12).
- Their own reading of the model: "our model does not have strong ion–ion correlations" (p. S-11).
- Relevance to our manuscript: provides the coarse-grained model we simulate (the parameters above are ours: l_B = e^2/(4 pi eps_0 eps_r k_B T) with eps_r = 7.5, sigma = 0.7 nm and T = 400 K gives 7.96 sigma). Their statement that ion-ion correlations are weak is worth contrasting with our kernels (sign-reversed at short range, Gamma up to about 9 at c = 0.08): the direct cation-anion pairing is indeed suppressed, but the polymer-mediated correlations are large. Volume and pages of the parent Letter must be looked up before citing.

### Liang2024 (ACS Macro Lett. 13, 1258)
- System: Li+-TFSI--PEO, Li:EO = 1:10 and 1:18/1:20, 300 and 373 K; OPLS force field with CL&P TFSI and Åqvist Li+ (their refs 29-31).
- Method: adaptive force matching of classical forces on Li+ to periodic DFT forces (five replicas, annealing between iterations); five parameters (charge scaling and four Li-O LJ parameters).
- Claims: the scaling factor converges to 0.79 for all conditions, objective 11% (Table 2, p. 1260); Li+ diffusivity improves by about one order of magnitude against experiment; t_Li drops from 0.28 to 0.21 (exp. 0.24); "the diffusivity of Li+ is dominated by electrostatic interactions" (p. 1259); Li+-environment energy falls from about -205 to -130 kcal/mol, matching first-principles values; SSIP fraction rises from 0.18 to 0.59 (1:10) and 0.31 to 0.85 (1:18) (p. 1260).
- Terminology warning: their "force matching" fits force-field parameters to quantum forces; ours fits a free-energy functional to the mean interaction force density of MD (Yvon-Born-Green). The manuscript should define our usage once.
- Relevance to our manuscript: shows that even atomistic models need an effective, environment-dependent electrostatics (a uniform 0.79 charge scaling stands in for polarisation and charge transfer); a learned functional absorbs exactly such effective electrostatics into its short-range kernels. Also the all-atom target for a future application of our method.

### Gouveia2017 (Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 19, 29617)
- System: [C2C1im] ionic liquids with FSI, BETI, TFSAM, TSAC, NF, TFSI, TF anions; densities and viscosities 293-353 K.
- Method: experiment plus re-parameterisation of the CL&P force field (OPLS-AA form) validated on liquid densities and crystal structures (GROMACS, DL_POLY).
- Claims: FSI and TFSAM give the lowest densities and viscosities; "Deviations of up to 5% in density between experimental and simulation results are common and considered acceptable" (p. 29618); the re-parameterised set removes the large density deviations seen for asymmetric anions (Fig. 5).
- Relevance to our manuscript: the current recommended TFSI/triflate parameters in the CL&P family (the atomistic anion model behind Thum2021 and Liang2024). Background only; no positioning content.

### Bui2026 (arXiv:2603.20493v2; PNAS 123, e2610049123 per Cheng2026 ref. 12)
- System: water (SCAN, RPBE-D3 MLIPs; TIP4P/2005) and CO2 (PBE-D3, BLYP-D3, SCAN-rVV10 MLIPs; TraPPE); MLIPs from DeepMD, HD-NNP, MACE.
- Method: "ab initio neural cDFT": canonical MD under random planar V_ext(z) (sines plus piecewise linear ramps, LJ 9-3 walls), 500-1000 simulations of 40-1024 molecules per potential, T randomised (220-500 K for CO2, 450-950 K for water); the neural c^(1)(z;[rho];T) is Sammüller-style local learning with a 1 nm window and three dense layers (128/64/32, softplus); the chemical potentials of the canonical runs are latent variables (their ref. 51, SammullerSchmidt2026); F_ex from functional line integration; grand potential, pressure, S(k) via OZ, coexistence by fixed mean-density minimisation.
- Claims: reproduces EOS, S(k), binodals (T_c 657 K TIP4P/2005, 584 K RPBE-D3, 695 K SCAN; CO2 300/299/331/343 K for TraPPE/PBE-D3/BLYP-D3/SCAN-rVV10, p. 4); confined water phase diagrams; Fisher-Widom and Widom lines of CO2 from the pole structure of 1 - rho c^(2)(k); each cDFT calculation takes about a minute, a 200 nm profile about an hour on a GPU (p. 3-4).
- Held-out claims: "no bulk simulation data—whether structural or thermodynamic (e.g., free energies, pressures, or chemical potentials)—were provided during training. The bulk equations of state and structure factors shown in Fig. 2B should therefore be regarded as genuine held-out predictions" (p. 6); "c^(1)(z;[rho];T)—an object defined in the grand canonical ensemble—is learned entirely from data generated in canonical MD simulations" (p. 6).
- Limitations they state: planar geometry only; short-ranged MLIPs so "explicit electrostatic interactions are missing" (p. 8); for long-ranged electrostatics they point to their ionic-fluid work, "we have shown how the effects of long-ranged electrostatics can be accurately accounted for using a well-controlled mean-field approximation [47]" (p. 9), and to hyper-DFT for molecular liquids.
- One-component only; mixtures are not treated.
- Relevance to our manuscript: the style reference (Results one subsection per figure, then Methods) and the closest precedent for training on canonical MD profiles under random planar potentials and reporting S(k) as a held-out prediction. Their electrostatics enters via LMFT mean field in a separate PRL (BuiCox2025), not in this paper.

### Cheng2026 (arXiv:2608.13506v1)
- System: Lennard-Jones truncated and shifted at 2.5 sigma, one component, 23 temperatures T = 0.625-1.8, L = 8 sigma cells, 16^3 grid at 0.5 sigma spacing, N = 8-464; 10,957 canonical MD fields under random 1D/2D/3D Gaussian external potentials; test temperatures 0.7, 1.1, 1.5 held out entirely.
- Method: "Equi-cDFT", F_exc[rho,T] = Delta V sum_g rho_g a_exc(chi_g,T), local environments encoded by O_h-invariant Cartesian moments (lattice CACE), 1,762 parameters; c^(1) by autodiff; loss = spatial variance of the local chemical potential mu_loc,g = ln(Lambda^3 rho_g) + beta V_ext,g - c^(1)_g (Eq. 8), which eliminates the unknown canonical mu analytically and leaves only the gauge F -> F + b(T) N; c^(2) is the unsupervised Hessian.
- Claims: forward and inverse tests at held-out T with RMSE 0.01-0.09; transfer to 3.4x larger cells and from NVT training to GCMC chemical-potential differences; S(k), compressibility-route EOS with van der Waals loops, coexistence (T_c 1.09, rho_c 0.31 versus 1.08, 0.32 from MD), interfacial broadening, "none of which are used as training targets" (abstract); 3D applications: solvent-mediated colloid force via the Feynman-Hellmann identity (Eq. 14) and gyroid adsorption isotherm.
- Positioning statements: "learned approximations have largely remained restricted to planar or lower-dimensional settings" (abstract); "For mixtures, the density of each component can be treated as a species-resolved input channel" (p. 8, future work); "Ionic and polar fluids require an additional long-ranged branch: local molecular field theory already provides one successful separation of learned short-range correlations from analytical electrostatics [11]. Alternatively, reciprocal-space, structure-factor-like features with Coulomb kernels could augment the local functional" (p. 8).
- Architecture note relevant to our ablation: the production model is "the sum of a pointwise baseline and a finite-range lattice-CACE correction" (Eq. 15, p. 9), i.e. also a pointwise nonlinearity plus a nonlocal term, but the two are not ablated against each other.
- Relevance to our manuscript: the second style reference; the nearest precedent for a canonical-ensemble, chemical-potential-free training signal (its local-balance loss is the integral form of our force-matching identity); explicitly lists mixtures and ionic fluids as open extensions.

### Avni2022 (Phys. Rev. Lett. 128, 098002)
- System: monovalent electrolytes (NaCl, KBr, LiI, KF, KCl) in water up to 3 M, 5-50 C; crystallographic radii; no fit parameters.
- Method: stochastic density functional theory (Dean-Kawasaki) for n_+/-(r) coupled to Stokes flow, linearised around the bulk; Coulomb replaced by the cut-off kernel V_co(r) = e^2 theta(r - a)/(4 pi eps_0 eps r) with a = r_+ + r_- (Eq. 9, p. 098002-3), justified because "a viable modification is to apply a cutoff to the Coulomb interaction [32]" and it "approximates very well (far better than the pure Coulomb interaction) the average distance between oppositely charged ions in a Coulomb gas with hard-core repulsion, for concentrations up to a few molars" (p. 098002-2/3); self-interaction subtracted by C -> C - n delta (Eq. 22).
- Claims: kappa = kappa_0 + kappa_hyd + kappa_el with closed integrals Eqs. 23-24 in which the kernel enters as cos(ax/lambda_D); reduces to Debye-Hückel-Onsager for a << lambda_D; agreement with experiment "up to 3 molars, without any fit parameters" (abstract), "the largest deviation being 14% for NaCl at 3M" (p. 098002-5).
- Note: the cut-off kernel is a static, species-independent, concentration-independent modification of the bare Coulomb interaction; in Fourier space it is proportional to cos(ka)/k^2 and changes sign, which is the mechanism behind the oscillatory charge correlations discussed in Adar2019 (not in this Letter).
- Relevance to our manuscript: the mean-field closure we go beyond. Its kernel is the natural analytic baseline for a learned ionic kernel; in our data it gives chi^2 = 73 at c = 0.04 and is unstable above c = 0.05 (result_summary, Claim 1). Our learned kernels do not merely cut the Coulomb interaction off at contact, they reverse its sign at short range and depend on concentration through the nonlinearity.

### Dijkman2025 (Phys. Rev. Lett. 134, 056103)
- System: supercritical LJ (r_cut = 4 sigma, k_B T/epsilon = 2), planar geometry, L = 10 sigma, Delta z = sigma/32; GCMC at beta mu in [-4, 0.5], rho sigma^3 in [0.02, 0.67]; 800 bulk g(r).
- Method: "pair-correlation matching": a CNN F_theta^(2)(rho_1..rho_n) (six dilated periodic conv layers, 24.4 K parameters) trained so that its Hessian matches the laterally integrated bulk direct correlation function obtained from g(r) via Ornstein-Zernike (Eqs. 2-3, A5-A6), plus an offset loss fixing delta F/delta rho at bulk density; compared with F_theta^(1) trained on 800 inhomogeneous profiles and with the van der Waals mean-field F_MF (White Bear II FMT plus attraction).
- Claims: predicts inhomogeneous rho(z) under 150 external potentials across -4 < beta mu < 6 as well as F^(1) inside the training range and better outside it; predicts bulk pressure, mu, c(z) and g(r); "circumventing the need to sample costly heterogeneous density profiles" (abstract); "existing training schemes of classical DFT ... so far have all been based on training on density profiles of inhomogeneous fluids in a variety of external fields" (p. 056103-4/5); notes that F^(2) is "more sensitive to highly inhomogeneous densities" (p. 056103-3).
- Data direction is the reverse of ours: they train on bulk pair correlations and predict profiles; we train on driven profiles and predict bulk structure factors.
- Relevance to our manuscript: prior neural cDFT without charges and the paper that makes the pair-correlation route explicit; its target is exactly the quantity (the Hessian, i.e. S(k)) that we hold out as a test.

## 3. What none of these papers does (with the honest caveats)

1. A learned excess functional for a charged binary mixture in a polymer host. Dijkman2025, Cheng2026 and Bui2026 treat one-component fluids (LJ, water, CO2); the polymer-electrolyte papers (Pesko2016, Timachova2015, Kuhlmann2026, Thum2021, Tsamopoulos2023, Liang2024) contain no functional at all; Avni2022 uses a fixed analytic kernel. Caveat: learned cDFT for ions does exist for the primitive model without solvent structure (BuiCox2025, cited within Bui2026 and Cheng2026), and binary neutral mixtures have been learned (Robitschko2025, cited within both). What is missing in the whole set is the polymer-mediated, size-asymmetric, two-species ionic case, where the solvent is a melt of chains and the coarse-grained ions are 0.4 and 1.6 sigma.

2. Analytic Coulomb mean field separated from the learned part. This is NOT new as a principle: Cheng2026 states that "local molecular field theory already provides one successful separation of learned short-range correlations from analytical electrostatics [11]" (p. 8), referring to BuiCox2025, and Bui2026 says the same (p. 9, ref. 47). Avni2022 is entirely analytic. Our manuscript should present the separation as following that precedent. What is different here: the learned part is an explicit, closed-form object (radial pair kernels on a Gaussian basis plus a pointwise map of Gaussian-smoothed densities) whose Fourier transform, Stillinger-Lovett limit and sum rules can be checked analytically (result_summary Claim 7), rather than a windowed neural c^(1) as in Sammuller2023/Bui2026 or a lattice-CACE readout as in Cheng2026.

3. Force matching in the canonical ensemble via the Yvon-Born-Green identity f^int = -n d_z mu^int. Partly new. Cheng2026 also trains on canonical MD without chemical-potential labels, using the spatial variance of the local chemical potential (its Eq. 8), which is mathematically the integrated form of the same balance; Bui2026 uses latent mu_k per run (SammullerSchmidt2026). Neither uses the mean interaction force density measured in MD as the regression target; Dijkman2025 uses bulk g(r); no paper in the set uses force densities. The honest statement: our target is a mechanical observable with its own block error bars, which lets the residual be quoted in units of the MD noise; the ensemble aspect itself has precedent. Also note the terminology clash with Liang2024's "adaptive force matching" (forces on Li+ against DFT).

4. Bulk structure factors and the thermodynamic factor as held-out predictions. For one-component fluids this is already done: Bui2026 reports S(k) of CO2 as a "genuine held-out prediction" (p. 6) and Cheng2026 reports S(k), EOS and coexistence with "none of these quantities entered the loss" (p. 8); Dijkman2025 reproduces g(r) but trains on it. None of the 13 papers reports the partial structure factors of a mixture, S_ZZ(k) and S_NN(k), or the thermodynamic factor 2n/S_NN, from a learned functional. Kuhlmann2026 names the thermodynamic factor as a required continuum-model input (p. 1598) but measures nothing of the kind. So the claim should be worded as "for a charged mixture", not as the first S(k) prediction.

5. The pair-kernel versus nonlinearity comparison across concentrations. No paper in the set makes it. Dijkman2025 compares two training objectives (F^(1) vs F^(2)) and a mean-field functional, not two functional forms; Cheng2026 builds a pointwise baseline plus a nonlocal correction (Eq. 15) but does not ablate the two; Avni2022 has a fixed kernel and no nonlinearity; the polymer-electrolyte papers have no functional. The finding that a learned density-independent pair kernel is exact at one concentration and fails across the sequence, with the concentration dependence living in the anion channel of the nonlinearity, has no counterpart in the set.

6. Ion-ion correlations in the Tsamopoulos-Wang model. Tsamopoulos2023 (SI, p. S-11) states that the model "does not have strong ion–ion correlations", based on the small cation-anion g(r) peak. Our kernels and Gamma(c) quantify what that model does have: strong polymer-mediated correlations that cancel the bare Coulomb attraction at contact. This is a physical finding about the same model, and it should be stated as consistent with (not contradicting) their g(r) observation.

### Introduction, paragraph i: polymer electrolytes and why ion correlations matter beyond mean field

Solvent-free polymer electrolytes carry lithium salts at concentrations of order one ion per ten monomers, far from the dilute limit [Kuhlmann2026, Pesko2016]. Their conductivity rises with salt content, passes through a maximum near [Li]/[EO] = 0.085 in poly(ethylene oxide), and falls as the salt stiffens the chains and screens the carriers [Pesko2016; Lascaud1994 cited within Pesko2016 ref. 15]. The cation is chelated by ether oxygens while the bulky anion is free, so the two ions diffuse at very different rates and their motions decouple with increasing salt [Timachova2015]. Continuum models of the cell need the conductivity, the salt diffusivity, the transference number and the thermodynamic factor as concentration-dependent inputs [Kuhlmann2026], and the electrolyte keeps its equilibrium structure under the fields of a working cell [Kuhlmann2026]. All-atom simulations resolve the ion coordination near electrodes, where layering produces free-energy barriers of several k_B T for the cation [Thum2021], but each new potential or composition requires a new microsecond trajectory, and even atomistic force fields need an effective, environment-dependent electrostatics: the lithium charge must be scaled by about 0.8 to reproduce measured diffusivities [Liang2024]. Coarse-grained bead-spring models retain what governs these effects, a solvating polymer, a small cation, a large anion and a low dielectric constant, and reproduce the glass-transition and conductivity trends of PEO/LiTFSI [Tsamopoulos2023; ShenHall2020 cited within Tsamopoulos2023 ref. S2]. In such a melt the Bjerrum length is about eight bead diameters, so the mean-field Poisson-Boltzmann description of the ion densities cannot hold: the correlations that a mean field discards are of the same size as the interactions it keeps. A quantitative theory of the ion densities in a polymer therefore needs a closure that encodes the polymer-mediated ion correlations, and none has been derived.

### Introduction, paragraph ii: learned classical density functional theory

Classical density functional theory expresses the equilibrium one-body densities of a fluid through an excess free-energy functional that is exact in principle and unknown in practice [Dijkman2025, Cheng2026]. Accurate functionals exist for hard spheres, and mean-field or mean-spherical closures are used for attractive and Coulombic interactions [Dijkman2025, Bui2026]. Machine learning has changed this. Early work fitted nonlocal corrections to planar density profiles [LinOettel2019 cited within Dijkman2025 ref. 11; Cats2021 cited within Dijkman2025 ref. 12]. Neural functional theory learns the one-body direct correlation function from grand-canonical simulations under random external potentials [Sammuller2023 cited within Dijkman2025 ref. 13], and has since been extended to temperature, phase coexistence and binary mixtures [Sammuller2025PRX and Robitschko2025 cited within Cheng2026 refs 19, 21]. Pair-correlation matching trains the Hessian of a neural functional on bulk radial distribution functions instead of profiles [Dijkman2025; SammullerSchmidt2024 cited within Dijkman2025 ref. 23]. Canonical molecular dynamics can replace grand-canonical sampling when the chemical potentials are treated as latent variables [SammullerSchmidt2026 cited within Bui2026 ref. 51] or eliminated by requiring a spatially uniform local chemical potential [Cheng2026]. On this basis neural functionals now reach first-principles accuracy for water and carbon dioxide [Bui2026] and full three-dimensional geometries with the equivariance of the density grid built in [Cheng2026]. For ions the long range of the Coulomb interaction is handled by keeping the electrostatic mean field analytic and learning only the short-range remainder, as in local molecular field theory [BuiCox2025 cited within Bui2026 ref. 47 and Cheng2026 ref. 11]. The alternative for concentrated electrolytes has been to modify the bare Coulomb kernel by hand: a hard cut-off at the contact distance gives a parameter-free conductivity theory that matches experiment to three molar [Avni2022; Adar2019 cited within Avni2022 ref. 32]. Structure factors, equations of state and coexistence curves have been shown to emerge from learned functionals of one-component fluids without entering the training [Bui2026, Cheng2026]. A learned functional for a two-species ionic fluid in a polymer, with the mixture structure factors as a test, has not been reported [Cheng2026].

### Introduction, paragraph iii: what we do

Here we learn the excess free-energy functional of the cation and anion densities of a coarse-grained salt-doped polymer melt [Tsamopoulos2023] from molecular dynamics. The Coulomb mean field is kept analytic; the learned part is a set of radial pair kernels on a Gaussian basis together with a pointwise neural nonlinearity of Gaussian-smoothed densities, so that translational and rotational covariance, integrability and the Stillinger-Lovett sum rule hold by construction. The functional is trained only by force matching: the Yvon-Born-Green identity relates the mean interaction force density measured in the simulation to the gradient of the excess chemical potential, and the residual is measured in units of the simulation noise. Training data are planar ion density profiles under static external potentials at salt concentrations c = N_+/N_bead = 0.01 to 0.08. The functional reproduces the density response to held-out potentials at the noise level, predicts an untrained concentration, and yields the charge and number structure factors and the thermodynamic factor 2n/S_NN(k_1) without any of them entering the training. A learned pair kernel alone fits one concentration but not the sequence of concentrations; the density dependence of the ion correlations lives in the nonlinearity, in the anion channel. The learned kernels reverse the sign of the bare Coulomb interaction at short range: the monomer solvation shell removes the cation-anion pairing, and the learned like-ion term is attractive, so that the total like-ion repulsion is far weaker than Coulomb.

### Discussion paragraph: our result relative to Avni's cut-off kernel and to the neural cDFT works

The cut-off Coulomb kernel of Avni and co-workers replaces the interaction inside the contact distance by zero and, with no adjustable parameter, reproduces the conductivity of aqueous salts to three molar [Avni2022]. In the polymer melt the same construction fails: with the contact distance set to one bead diameter its force residual is two orders of magnitude above the noise at c = 0.04 (chi^2 = 73) and its uniform state is unstable above c = 0.05 (the inverse structure matrix acquires a negative eigenvalue). The learned kernels show why. Inside one bead diameter they do not vanish; they invert the bare interaction, so that the total cation-anion potential is flat inside one bead diameter instead of -40 k_B T and the like-ion repulsion is reduced to a few k_B T. This is the signature of a solvation shell that the polymer builds around the small cation, consistent with the weak direct cation-anion pairing seen in the radial distribution functions of this model [Tsamopoulos2023]. A fixed kernel of either sign is not enough, because the kernel that fits one concentration misses the others; the concentration dependence enters through the pointwise nonlinearity, and only in the anion channel. Relative to neural functionals for one-component fluids, our construction keeps the electrostatics analytic in the spirit of local molecular field theory [BuiCox2025 cited within Bui2026 ref. 47], trains in the canonical ensemble without chemical-potential labels as in the local-balance scheme of [Cheng2026], and holds out the bulk pair structure that pair-correlation matching uses as its training signal [Dijkman2025]. The mixture structure factors and the thermodynamic factor then serve as tests of the learned Hessian in the same way that S(k) and the equation of state test the one-component functionals of [Bui2026, Cheng2026]. The extension to species-resolved density channels that those works anticipate [Cheng2026] is realised here for two charged species, at the price of a planar geometry and a concentration range that ends at c = 0.08.
