# Project Truth

## Core Story
- Central claim: A neural excess free-energy functional for the ions of a salt-doped polymer melt, built from radial kernels and pointwise maps with the Coulomb part analytic, for which E(3) covariance, integrability, the Noether sum rules and the Stillinger-Lovett condition hold for every parameter value (proofs in `paper/input/closure_note.md`), trained only by force matching on planar MD density profiles, reproduces the ion density response to external potentials at the MD noise level across salt concentrations c = 0.01-0.08, predicts an untrained concentration, and yields the bulk structure factors and the thermodynamic factor; the pointwise nonlinearity is what carries the concentration dependence of the ion correlations (a learned pair kernel alone cannot), and the number-channel response is reproduced down to the longest wavelength of the box, k_1 = 2 pi / L. At four times the Bjerrum length (eps_r = 2, trained on its own data with the same architecture) the pair kernel fails outright while the functional stays at the noise level and follows a Gamma whose excess Gamma - 1 changes sign with concentration.
- Venue target: PNAS Research Article (user decision 2026-09-29), Physical Sciences; 6 printed pages standard (about 4,000 words with about four medium display items), extra pages at a charge; Abstract <= 250 words, Significance Statement <= 120 words; SI Appendix for proofs, details and ablations. Nature-style prose rules apply.
- Article type: research article: Introduction (no heading), a section "A structure-preserving neural density functional" (Fig. 1, Table 1), Results (four subsections, Figs 2-5), Discussion, Materials and Methods; Fig. 1 is the model schematic.
- Dominant contribution: method (structure-preserving learned closure for an ionic mixture in a polymer), with one physical finding (polymer-mediated ion correlations: sign-reversed short-range kernels, density dependence in the anion channel, long-range number-channel kernel above c = 0.04).
- Active manuscript: none yet. `paper/output/doc/manuscript_outline.md` is the drafting skeleton; `paper/notes/result_summary.md` and `paper/notes/paper_handoff.md` are the source of truth for numbers until a draft exists. All numbers trace to `runs/learn/*/metrics.json` and `runs/learn/summary.txt`.

## Open Risks
- The strongest c = 0.08 wells (p01, contrast ~100) are fitted at chi^2 22 by the best model (validation run): the highest local densities are the weak spot inside the data range.
- eps_r = 2, c = 0.01: the functional's Gamma is 1.9 sigma low and its profiles are the least accurate of any state point (partly MD noise: the error bars alone give 5-6% relative L2); p06 is a real 14% deviation. State it in the Fig. 3 caption and Results 2.
- The Gaussian-basis extrapolation below k_1 and above c = 0.08 is not constrained by data; text must not claim it.
- Models trained before the long-wavelength runs existed (the m >= 3-only fits) are not shown or mentioned anywhere in the manuscript, figures, captions or supplement (user decision 2026-09-26). The paper describes one data set, the 106 accepted runs at the five training concentrations (128 with c = 0.05; corrected from 92 on 2026-09-29), and one training.
- Error bars of the force profiles come from 8 blocks and are conservative (YBG chi^2 0.5-0.8 on the raw data), so chi^2 = 0.6 is the noise floor, not 1.0; state this once.
